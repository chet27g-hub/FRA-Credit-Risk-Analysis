Research question

PROJECT THESIS

Was credit stress driven by the lender or the economy?

This study compares the asset-quality paths of a large private-sector bank and a lending NBFC, then asks whether stress was broad-based or institution-specific — and whether equity-market performance reflected that stress.

Current evidence boundary: the working dataset contains selected company ratios and annual share-price history; event-day abnormal returns and some peer metrics have separate source limitations. The dashboard therefore separates observed data from questions that still require evidence.
Study entities: ICICI Bank (private-sector bank) and Bajaj Finance (lending NBFC). Proposed peers are listed in Peer Benchmarking.

What the current ratios suggest

DESCRIPTIVE, NOT CAUSAL
  • ICICI Bank's working GNPA ratio peaks at 10.4% in FY2018 and declines to 1.4% by FY2026.
  • ICICI's working NNPA ratio declines from 5.4% in FY2017 to 0.33% in FY2026.
  • Bajaj Finance's working NNPA ratio rises to 0.75% in FY2021, then declines to 0.34% in FY2023 and is 0.41% in FY2026.
  • These patterns are consistent with improvement in the selected asset-quality ratios, but do not by themselves establish why the changes occurred.

Asset Quality & Credit Risk

GNPA ratio

FY17–FY26

How to interpret these measures

GNPA

Gross loans classified as non-performing before eligible provisions are deducted.

NNPA

Net NPA after deducting eligible provisions/adjustments under the applicable reporting definition.

PCR

Provision coverage ratio: provisions held against NPAs divided by GNPA, multiplied by 100, subject to company definition.

Slippages

Fresh NPA additions during a period. A slippage ratio relates new NPA accretions to standard advances.

03 / Risk attribution

Systematic vs Idiosyncratic Risk

Systematic / sector-wide drivers

Regulatory and macro shocks affecting multiple lenders. These are potential transmission channels, not proof that a specific NPA movement was caused by the event.

27 Mar 2020

RBI COVID-19 regulatory package — loan moratorium

RBI permitted a three-month moratorium on instalments due 1 March–31 May 2020 for term loans, including retail loans and NBFC lending. It altered repayment schedules and complicates delinquency comparisons. RBI circular

6 Aug 2020

RBI Resolution Framework for COVID-19 stress

Eligible stressed borrower accounts could be restructured under prescribed conditions while retaining standard classification. Analyse restructured books and subsequent slippages alongside GNPA. RBI framework

22 Oct 2021

Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) for NBFCs

RBI introduced a four-layer framework with enhanced governance, capital, prudential and disclosure requirements calibrated to NBFC size and risk. Relevant to Bajaj Finance as a large NBFC; primarily affects controls and compliance expectations. RBI framework

2 Sep 2022

RBI Digital Lending Guidelines

Clarified regulated-entity accountability for lending service providers, borrower disclosures, fund flows, grievance redressal and data practices. Relevant to digital sourcing and consumer lending by banks and NBFCs. RBI guidelines

16 Nov 2023

Higher risk weights on unsecured consumer credit

RBI raised risk weights on specified consumer-credit exposures of banks and NBFCs by 25 percentage points to 125%; credit-card receivables also received higher weights. This increased capital intensity and could moderate growth or change product mix. RBI circular

25 Feb 2025

RBI restores risk weights on bank exposures to NBFCs

From 1 April 2025, banks' exposures to NBFCs reverted to external-rating-based risk weights, removing the additional 25-point add-on for specified exposures. This may ease a funding-channel constraint; it does not reverse higher risk weights on NBFC consumer-credit assets. RBI circular

8 May 2025

RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2025

Consolidated earlier instructions and added transparency requirements for loan products aggregated from multiple lenders and reporting of digital lending apps for a public directory. Relevant to customer acquisition, partner oversight and conduct risk. RBI directions

Macro cycle

Interest rates, inflation and borrower cash flows

Higher borrowing costs or weaker household/business cash flows can increase repayment stress. Test against policy-rate changes, income indicators and sector-level delinquency data.

Idiosyncratic / company-specific drivers

Documented institution-level events that may affect one lender more than peers. Regulatory action is not automatically evidence of deterioration in loan-asset quality.

5 Jan 2021

RBI penalty on Bajaj Finance — recovery practices

RBI imposed a ₹2.50 crore penalty after finding failures relating to recovery-agent conduct, customer harassment/intimidation and fair-practices/outsourcing requirements. This is conduct and operational risk, not proof of an NPA event. RBI press release

15 Nov 2023

RBI restricts two Bajaj Finance digital products

RBI directed Bajaj Finance to stop sanction and disbursal under eCOM and Insta EMI Card due to non-issuance/deficiencies in Key Fact Statements under digital-lending rules. This directly affected product origination and highlighted compliance and customer-disclosure risk. RBI press release

2 May 2024

RBI lifts Bajaj Finance product restrictions

RBI lifted the eCOM and digital Insta EMI Card restrictions after remedial actions. Treat this as resolution of the 2023 supervisory restriction; do not assume an immediate credit-quality effect. Company regulatory update

2 May 2025

RBI penalty on ICICI Bank — compliance deficiencies

RBI imposed a ₹97.80 lakh penalty relating to cyber-security incident reporting, account-alert software and credit-card statement/late-charge compliance. This is operational, cyber and conduct risk, not a direct credit-loss finding. RBI press release

Ongoing

Underwriting, concentration and model risk

Rapid growth, weaker borrower selection, concentration in customer/product segments, fraud and model drift can increase credit losses. Test using segment-level AUM growth, delinquencies, slippages, write-offs and vintage performance disclosed by each lender.

How to determine whether risk was systematic or idiosyncratic

  1. Identify the year in which GNPA, NNPA, slippages or credit cost deteriorated.
  2. Compare the same metric for the company's peers over the same period.
  3. If peers deteriorate in a similar direction and magnitude, the stress may be sector-wide.
  4. If the selected company deteriorates materially more than peers, investigate company-specific exposures, underwriting, governance or policy events.
  5. Use annual-report disclosures and credible event evidence before assigning a cause.
The present dataset shows ICICI's GNPA ratio peaking in FY2018 and declining thereafter, and Bajaj Finance's NNPA ratio peaking in FY2021. Without peer time series and event-level disclosures, the causes cannot be conclusively classified from these ratios alone.

06 / Data integrity

Methodology & Sources

Data integrity statement: This site uses the working series consolidated in the supplied Markdown file. The file itself warns that the figures are not a fully audited or complete 10-year database and that some values have not been independently reconciled to annual reports. Treat the displayed figures as provisional until verified.

Data-break controls

  • Separate standalone and consolidated reporting.
  • Record Ind AS / previous accounting-basis transitions.
  • Check NPA recognition and provisioning definition changes.
  • Document mergers, acquisitions and portfolio transfers.
  • Distinguish gross versus net write-offs and recoveries.
  • Record changes in advances, AUM or average loan-book definitions.

Calculation conventions

  • PCR = provisions held against NPAs ÷ GNPA × 100, subject to company definition.
  • Slippage ratio = fresh NPA additions ÷ standard advances × 100, using source definition.
  • Credit cost = credit impairment/provisions charged ÷ average loan book × 100, if this matches disclosed definition.
  • Annual return = ending adjusted price ÷ beginning adjusted price − 1.
  • Never replace missing observations with zero.
