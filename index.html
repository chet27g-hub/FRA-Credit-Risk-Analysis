01 / Research framework

Can two successful lenders face the same shock and experience different credit-risk outcomes?

The project separates institution-specific risk from economy-wide and sector-wide stress, compares each lender with its own peer group, and tests whether market prices reflect credit-risk information.

RESEARCH QUESTION 01

Credit-risk performance

How did asset quality, provisioning, slippages and credit cost evolve relative to appropriate peers?

RESEARCH QUESTION 02

Risk attribution

Which outcomes reflect common shocks, sector transmission or institution-specific decisions?

RESEARCH QUESTION 03

Market reaction

Did share prices respond to credit-risk events beyond broad market movements?

04 / Systematic and idiosyncratic risk

Systematic vs Idiosyncratic Risk

Filters the event timeline below; source links remain attached to each event.

Systematic / sector-wide drivers

Regulatory and macro shocks affecting multiple lenders. These are potential transmission channels, not proof that a specific NPA movement was caused by the event.

27 Mar 2020

RBI COVID-19 regulatory package — loan moratorium

RBI permitted a three-month moratorium on instalments due 1 March–31 May 2020 for term loans, including retail loans and NBFC lending. It altered repayment schedules and complicates delinquency comparisons. RBI circular

6 Aug 2020

RBI Resolution Framework for COVID-19 stress

Eligible stressed borrower accounts could be restructured under prescribed conditions while retaining standard classification. Analyse restructured books and subsequent slippages alongside GNPA. RBI framework

22 Oct 2021

Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) for NBFCs

RBI introduced a four-layer framework with enhanced governance, capital, prudential and disclosure requirements calibrated to NBFC size and risk. Relevant to Bajaj Finance as a large NBFC; primarily affects controls and compliance expectations. RBI framework

2 Sep 2022

RBI Digital Lending Guidelines

Clarified regulated-entity accountability for lending service providers, borrower disclosures, fund flows, grievance redressal and data practices. Relevant to digital sourcing and consumer lending by banks and NBFCs. RBI guidelines

16 Nov 2023

Higher risk weights on unsecured consumer credit

RBI raised risk weights on specified consumer-credit exposures of banks and NBFCs by 25 percentage points to 125%; credit-card receivables also received higher weights. This increased capital intensity and could moderate growth or change product mix. RBI circular

25 Feb 2025

RBI restores risk weights on bank exposures to NBFCs

From 1 April 2025, banks' exposures to NBFCs reverted to external-rating-based risk weights, removing the additional 25-point add-on for specified exposures. This may ease a funding-channel constraint; it does not reverse higher risk weights on NBFC consumer-credit assets. RBI circular

8 May 2025

RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2025

Consolidated earlier instructions and added transparency requirements for loan products aggregated from multiple lenders and reporting of digital lending apps for a public directory. Relevant to customer acquisition, partner oversight and conduct risk. RBI directions

Macro cycle

Interest rates, inflation and borrower cash flows

Higher borrowing costs or weaker household/business cash flows can increase repayment stress. Test against policy-rate changes, income indicators and sector-level delinquency data.

Idiosyncratic / company-specific drivers

Documented institution-level events that may affect one lender more than peers. Regulatory action is not automatically evidence of deterioration in loan-asset quality.

5 Jan 2021

RBI penalty on Bajaj Finance — recovery practices

RBI imposed a ₹2.50 crore penalty after finding failures relating to recovery-agent conduct, customer harassment/intimidation and fair-practices/outsourcing requirements. This is conduct and operational risk, not proof of an NPA event. RBI press release

15 Nov 2023

RBI restricts two Bajaj Finance digital products

RBI directed Bajaj Finance to stop sanction and disbursal under eCOM and Insta EMI Card due to non-issuance/deficiencies in Key Fact Statements under digital-lending rules. This directly affected product origination and highlighted compliance and customer-disclosure risk. RBI press release

2 May 2024

RBI lifts Bajaj Finance product restrictions

RBI lifted the eCOM and digital Insta EMI Card restrictions after remedial actions. Treat this as resolution of the 2023 supervisory restriction; do not assume an immediate credit-quality effect. Bajaj Finance FY25 management discussion

3 May 2021

RBI penalty on ICICI Bank — investment portfolio norms

RBI imposed a ₹3 crore penalty for contravention of prudential directions on classification, valuation and operation of the investment portfolio. This is regulatory-compliance risk, not evidence of a loan-NPA event. RBI press release

2 May 2025

RBI penalty on ICICI Bank — cyber, KYC and card conduct controls

RBI imposed a ₹97.80 lakh penalty after supervisory findings concerning cyber-incident reporting, account-alert software and credit-card billing/late-charge requirements. Classify as operational, cyber and conduct-compliance risk, not direct evidence of credit losses or GNPA deterioration. RBI press release

8 Aug 2025

RBI penalty on ICICI Bank — property valuation and current-account rules

RBI imposed a ₹75 lakh penalty for non-compliance relating to independent property valuations in certain mortgage loans and current-account discipline. Treat this as process and regulatory-control risk; do not infer a quantified credit-loss effect without loan-level evidence. RBI press release

Ongoing

Underwriting, concentration and model risk

Rapid growth, weaker borrower selection, concentration in customer/product segments, fraud and model drift can increase credit losses. Test using segment-level AUM growth, delinquencies, slippages, write-offs and vintage performance disclosed by each lender.

How to determine whether risk was systematic or idiosyncratic

  1. Identify the year in which GNPA, NNPA, slippages or credit cost deteriorated.
  2. Compare the same metric for the company's peers over the same period.
  3. If peers deteriorate in a similar direction and magnitude, the stress may be sector-wide.
  4. If the selected company deteriorates materially more than peers, investigate company-specific exposures, underwriting, governance or policy events.
  5. Use annual-report disclosures and credible event evidence before assigning a cause.
The present dataset shows ICICI's GNPA ratio peaking in FY2018 and declining thereafter, and Bajaj Finance's NNPA ratio peaking in FY2021. Without peer time series and event-level disclosures, the causes cannot be conclusively classified from these ratios alone.
05 / Equity-market response

Market Reaction & Correlation

The assignment asks whether share prices reflected systematic and idiosyncratic credit-risk events. A defensible answer requires verified daily prices, corporate-action treatment and a matched benchmark.

Market-data decision: The previous annual close series and annual event-return table did not have a reproducible source-to-cell audit. They have been removed from analytical charts and conclusions. No CAGR, volatility, drawdown, abnormal return, correlation coefficient or R² is published from those unverified inputs.
Required price basis

Daily exchange prices, split/bonus-adjusted and dividend treatment explicitly stated. Total-return performance requires a verified dividend-reinvested series.

Event-study design

Pre-specify an estimation window and event windows (for example −5 to +5 trading sessions), then calculate market-model abnormal and cumulative abnormal returns.

Correlation design

Align monthly or quarterly credit-risk changes with stock returns; report sample size, lag choice, coefficient, R² where justified and diagnostics.

06 / Research answers and methodology

Conclusions & Methodology

What the evidence supports

The conclusions below are deliberately limited to sourced observations. Historical causal claims and market-event findings remain open until the full time series and daily prices are validated.

ANSWER 01 · CREDIT RISK

Different paths, different accounting contexts

ICICI Bank standalone GNPA improved from 1.73% in FY25 to 1.44% in FY26; NNPA improved from 0.42% to 0.35%, while PCR eased from 76.2% to 75.8%. Bajaj Finance standalone GNPA moved up from 1.18% to 1.27%, while NNPA improved from 0.56% to 0.52%; Stage 3 PCR was 60% in FY26. These observations do not alone identify the causes.

ANSWER 02 · RISK DRIVERS

Common shocks meet different exposures

COVID-19 was a broad economy-wide shock. IL&FS originated as institution-specific stress and transmitted liquidity pressure across parts of the NBFC sector. The severity of credit outcomes depends on borrower mix, underwriting, collections, funding and management response. The current dataset does not yet quantify each channel's causal contribution.

ANSWER 03 · MARKET REACTION

Market reaction not yet quantified

The previous annual close series was not reproducibly sourced and has been removed. Three secondary-source trading snapshots show a sharp intraday fall and subsequent recovery for Bajaj Finance after the November 2023 restrictions, a roughly 7.5% early-session rise after the May 2024 lifting, and a 0.21% fall in ICICI Bank on the first session after its May 2025 penalty while the Sensex rose. These are unadjusted observations, not abnormal returns. No statistical-significance or credit-risk/return correlation claim is made until daily adjusted prices and matched benchmarks are available.

CROSS-INSTITUTION INSIGHT

Do not declare a universal winner

A deposit-funded bank and a diversified lending NBFC have different funding models, regulatory metrics, borrower mixes and accounting disclosures. Credit-risk analysis must combine asset quality, growth, slippages, provisions, write-offs, credit cost and profitability, with a clear reporting basis for every number.

Data-break controls

  • Separate standalone and consolidated reporting.
  • Record Ind AS / previous accounting-basis transitions.
  • Check NPA recognition and provisioning definition changes.
  • Document mergers, acquisitions and portfolio transfers.
  • Distinguish gross versus net write-offs and recoveries.
  • Record changes in advances, AUM or average loan-book definitions.

Calculation conventions

  • PCR = provisions held against NPAs ÷ GNPA × 100, subject to company definition.
  • Slippage ratio = fresh NPA additions ÷ standard advances × 100, using source definition.
  • Credit cost = credit impairment/provisions charged ÷ average loan book × 100, if this matches disclosed definition.
  • Annual return = ending adjusted price ÷ beginning adjusted price − 1, only after corporate-action and dividend treatment are verified.
  • Never replace missing observations with zero.
