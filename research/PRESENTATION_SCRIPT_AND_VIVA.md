# FRA Group Project 2 — 15-minute presentation script and viva preparation

**Research title:** Credit Risk Intelligence — ICICI Bank vs Bajaj Finance  
**Research cut-off:** 16 November 2026  
**Important presentation rule:** Do not present the legacy FY2017–FY2025 peer charts or annual share-price observations as audited results. Use the FY2026 sourced snapshot for exact peer figures and state the historical-data limitations clearly.

## 15-minute spoken presentation

### 0:00–1:00 | Opening and research questions

Good morning. Our project is titled *Credit Risk Intelligence: ICICI Bank versus Bajaj Finance*. We examine how two important Indian lending institutions managed credit risk over a decade, how their outcomes compared with similar lenders, and what the market response can tell us.

Our central question is: can two successful lenders face the same economic shock and experience different credit-risk outcomes?

We answer three questions. First, how did credit risk evolve relative to appropriate peers? Second, were the risks systematic, sector-wide or institution-specific? Third, how did equity markets react to credit-risk information?

We selected a private-sector bank and a lending NBFC because their different funding structures, customer segments and regulatory measures provide a useful comparison. We do not treat them as identical institutions or attempt to declare one a universal winner.

### 1:00–3:00 | Institution selection and peer groups

ICICI Bank is a scheduled commercial bank regulated by the Reserve Bank of India. Its peer group comprises HDFC Bank, Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank. These are private-sector banks with deposit-funded balance sheets, retail and corporate lending activities, and comparable RBI asset-quality reporting.

Bajaj Finance is an RBI-regulated lending NBFC. Its peer group comprises Cholamandalam Investment and Finance, Shriram Finance and Mahindra Finance. These companies operate in consumer, vehicle, SME and other lending segments, but their portfolio mixes and disclosure conventions differ.

This distinction is important. Bank GNPA and NNPA ratios follow RBI bank asset-classification rules. NBFCs may disclose RBI GNPA and NNPA as well as Ind AS Gross Stage 3 and Net Stage 3. Those measures must not be treated as interchangeable.

At 31 March 2026, ICICI Bank reported standalone advances of ₹15,53,893 crore. Bajaj Finance reported standalone AUM of ₹3,72,986 crore. These are different measures—bank advances and NBFC AUM—so they describe scale but are not a like-for-like size ranking.

### 3:00–5:00 | FY2026 peer snapshot

Let us first look at the FY2026 standalone bank snapshot.

ICICI Bank reported GNPA of 1.44%, NNPA of 0.35% and provision coverage of 75.8%. HDFC Bank reported 1.15%, 0.38% and 67.2%, respectively. Axis Bank reported 1.23%, 0.37% and 70%. Kotak Mahindra Bank reported 1.20%, 0.25% and 79%.

This tells us that ICICI had a lower NNPA than HDFC and Axis, but a higher GNPA than the three peers shown. Kotak had the lowest NNPA and the highest reported PCR in this snapshot. No bank leads every measure.

For Bajaj Finance, the standalone FY2026 filing reported GNPA of 1.27%, NNPA of 0.52%, Stage 3 provision coverage of 60%, and AUM of ₹3,72,986 crore.

Cholamandalam reported RBI GNPA of 4.36% and NNPA of 2.87%, while separately reporting Ind AS Gross Stage 3 of 3.05% and Net Stage 3 of 1.61%. Shriram Finance reported Stage 3-based GNPA and NNPA ratios of 4.58% and 2.33%, with NPA provision coverage of 50.34%. Mahindra Finance reported Gross Stage 3 of 3.4%.

These figures indicate that Bajaj's reported standalone GNPA and NNPA are lower than the impaired-asset ratios shown for the selected NBFC peers. However, Chola and Mahindra use separate Stage 3 measures, and Shriram defines its NPA ratios using Stage 3 loans. The comparison is informative but not perfectly like-for-like.

### 5:00–8:00 | Growth and credit-risk performance

Credit risk should never be evaluated through GNPA alone.

GNPA measures the stock of gross non-performing loans. NNPA reflects the residual exposure after eligible provisions. Provision coverage indicates the extent to which the lender has provided against identified non-performing assets. Slippages measure fresh accounts entering NPA status during a period. Recoveries and upgrades reduce the stock of NPAs, while write-offs remove loans from the balance sheet but do not necessarily mean the borrower has repaid.

Credit cost measures the flow of impairment or provisioning expense relative to a defined loan-book or asset denominator. Its precise denominator varies, so the analyst must use the company's stated methodology.

For ICICI Bank, the ten-year standalone GNPA series shows the ratio rising from 7.89% in FY2017 to 8.84% in FY2018, before declining to 6.70% in FY2019 and 1.44% in FY2026. The FY2025 to FY2026 comparison shows a further reduction from 1.73% to 1.44%, or 0.29 percentage points. NNPA declined from 4.89% in FY2017 to 4.77% in FY2018 and 2.06% in FY2019, then reached 0.35% in FY2026. From FY2025 to FY2026 it declined from 0.42% to 0.35%, a reduction of 0.07 percentage points. PCR moved from 76.2% to 75.8%, a decline of 0.4 percentage points.

The direction is positive for gross and net asset quality, while coverage declined slightly. This does not prove why the improvement occurred. We would need to reconcile fresh slippages, recoveries, upgrades, write-offs, portfolio growth and other adjustments to understand the movement.

For Bajaj Finance, standalone GNPA increased from 1.18% in FY2025 to 1.27% in FY2026, an increase of 0.09 percentage points. NNPA declined from 0.56% to 0.52%, a reduction of 0.04 percentage points. Standalone AUM increased from ₹3,08,832 crore to ₹3,72,986 crore, approximately 20.8% growth.

Reported loan losses and provisions rose from ₹7,027 crore to ₹9,290 crore, approximately 32.2%. However, the FY2026 filing discusses additional ECL provisions and a presentation change. Before those additional ECL provisions and the presentation change, the FY2026 figure was ₹8,523 crore. Therefore, the entire increase should not automatically be interpreted as deterioration in borrower quality.

The key lesson is that rapid lending growth must be considered together with asset quality, provisions, portfolio composition and collection performance. ICICI's gross NPA stock bridge reconciles exactly. FY2025: ₹27,962 crore opening + ₹20,211 crore additions − ₹11,763 crore recoveries/upgrades/others − ₹9,270 crore write-offs − ₹2,974 crore NPA sales = ₹24,166 crore closing. FY2026: ₹24,166 crore + ₹19,147 crore − ₹11,492 crore − ₹8,424 crore − ₹345 crore = ₹23,052 crore. Q2 FY2027: ₹23,847 crore + ₹4,833 crore − ₹3,620 crore − ₹1,592 crore − ₹133 crore = ₹23,335 crore. The Q2 FY27 ratio is on a customer-assets basis, whereas FY26 standalone statements report GNPA on gross advances; these denominators should not be compared directly.

### 8:00–11:00 | Systematic and idiosyncratic risk

We classify risk into three levels.

Systematic risk is an economy-wide shock. COVID-19 is an example because lockdowns affected household income, business cash flows, collections and repayment capacity across the financial system. The 2016–2018 banking NPA recognition cycle also exposed system-wide corporate-credit stress: ICICI Bank's standalone GNPA rose from 7.89% in FY2017 to 8.84% in FY2018 before easing to 6.70% in FY2019.

Sector-wide risk affects a group of lenders through a common funding or asset-quality channel. The IL&FS crisis originated with institution-specific problems but transmitted liquidity stress across parts of the NBFC sector. RBI records the August 2018 commercial-paper defaults, subsequent market risk aversion, higher funding costs and loss of market access for some NBFCs. It is therefore best described as institution-specific in origin with wider sector contagion.

Idiosyncratic risk arises from a lender's own portfolio, underwriting, governance, operational controls or compliance decisions.

For ICICI Bank, the Videocon controversy raised conflict-of-interest and credit-approval oversight concerns. The independent enquiry found code-of-conduct and disclosure/recusal violations; the bank stated the report had no implications for published financial statements. The loan itself became an NPA, but the full sanctioned amount must not be described as a realised loss. For Bajaj Finance, FY2025 consolidated loan-loss provisions rose 72% to ₹7,966 crore from ₹4,631 crore while AUM grew 26%. Management attributed this to macro deterioration, rising leverage on unsecured loans and broader banking-sector stress. This is a credit-cost signal, not a 72% increase in realised defaults. RBI's November 2023 restrictions on eCOM and Insta EMI Card disbursals related to deficiencies in Key Fact Statements under digital-lending requirements. RBI lifted the restrictions in May 2024 after remedial actions. This is a compliance and product-origination event; it should not be presented as proof of borrower defaults.

For ICICI Bank, the RBI's May 2021 penalty related to prudential directions on classification, valuation and operation of the investment portfolio. This is regulatory-compliance risk, not evidence of a loan-NPA event.

The analytical chain is: shock, borrower or funding exposure, credit quality, provisions, profitability and investor response. Each link requires evidence. We should not assume that an event caused a change in GNPA simply because the dates are close.

### 11:00–13:00 | Stock-market reaction

The market section asks whether investors reacted to credit-risk information.

A proper analysis requires daily adjusted prices, corporate-action adjustments, a suitable benchmark, and precisely dated events. We also found three reported trading snapshots: Bajaj Finance fell about 4% intraday and later recovered after the November 2023 restrictions; it rose about 7.5% in early trade after the restrictions were lifted in May 2024; and ICICI Bank declined 0.21% on the first session after its May 2025 penalty while the Sensex rose 0.37%. These are unadjusted reported observations, not abnormal returns. For each event, we should calculate pre-event, event-window and post-event returns, compare them with a benchmark, and estimate abnormal returns. A market model or another justified expected-return method can then be used to calculate cumulative abnormal returns.

We must also calculate volatility and maximum drawdown from a consistent daily series. For Bajaj Finance, a broader financial-services or NBFC benchmark may be more appropriate than automatically using Nifty Bank.

The prior annual closing-price series was removed from the dashboard because it lacked a reproducible source-to-cell and corporate-action audit. Therefore, we do not claim verified CAGR, event-day abnormal returns, statistically significant correlations or causation from the three secondary-source snapshots. The dashboard explicitly withholds those conclusions until daily adjusted market data is validated.

This is a research limitation, not a reason to manufacture a result.

### 13:00–15:00 | Conclusions

We can now answer the three research questions.

First, on credit-risk performance, FY2026 disclosures show ICICI Bank's standalone GNPA and NNPA improving year on year, while Bajaj Finance's standalone GNPA increased slightly and NNPA declined. The peer snapshot shows that relative performance differs by metric, and NBFC reporting definitions require careful treatment.

Second, on risk drivers, the 2016–2018 banking NPA recognition cycle exposed corporate-credit stress; IL&FS transmitted liquidity stress across parts of the NBFC sector; COVID-19 was a systematic shock; and monetary tightening raised borrower and funding costs. ICICI's Videocon controversy illustrates governance risk, while Bajaj's digital-lending restrictions illustrate compliance risk. Bajaj's FY25 provisioning spike shows how macro deterioration and unsecured leverage can raise expected credit losses. These channels differ, and we do not claim one event caused all NPA movements.

Third, on market reaction, three secondary-source snapshots show short-term unadjusted price moves after selected regulatory events. They provide context but not a defensible event study. Daily adjusted prices and matched benchmark returns are needed before we can claim abnormal returns or correlation with credit risk.

The central lesson is that credit risk cannot be understood through one ratio. Loan growth, borrower concentration, slippages, provisioning, write-offs, credit cost and management decisions must be examined together. Economic shocks may affect an entire sector, but the severity of the outcome depends partly on each institution's exposures and risk-management practices. Market prices add another perspective, but they do not replace fundamental credit-risk analysis.

Thank you.

## Faculty viva — 26 questions and evidence-based answers

### 1. Why did you select ICICI Bank?

ICICI is a large private-sector scheduled commercial bank with deposit-funded lending, retail and corporate exposures, and a long history of RBI asset-quality disclosures. It allows comparison with other private-sector banks using broadly similar GNPA and NNPA conventions.

### 2. Why did you select Bajaj Finance?

Bajaj Finance is a major lending NBFC with diversified consumer, SME and commercial lending. It provides a contrasting business and funding model to a bank and offers a useful case for studying growth, provisions and retail-credit risk.

### 3. Why these peers?

HDFC Bank, Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank are private-sector bank peers. Cholamandalam, Shriram Finance and Mahindra Finance are lending NBFC peers. The groups share broad regulatory and lending characteristics, although their portfolio mixes and disclosures are not identical.

### 4. Why not include public-sector banks in ICICI's peer group?

The project focuses on a private-sector bank peer set. Public-sector banks have different ownership, governance and policy contexts. They can be included in a wider sector study, but they are not the closest direct peer group for this comparison.

### 5. What is the difference between GNPA and NNPA?

GNPA is the gross amount of non-performing loans before eligible provisions. NNPA is the residual non-performing exposure after eligible provisions and other prescribed deductions. NNPA therefore reflects the remaining net exposure more directly.

### 6. What does PCR tell you?

PCR measures provisions held against NPAs relative to gross NPAs, subject to the institution's disclosed definition. A higher PCR may indicate a larger provision buffer, but it does not by itself establish overall credit quality or future loss experience.

### 7. Can GNPA decline even when credit stress remains?

Yes. Recoveries, upgrades, write-offs, portfolio growth and changes in classification can reduce the GNPA ratio. Fresh slippages and the absolute amount of NPAs must be examined alongside the ratio.

### 8. What are slippages?

Slippages are fresh loans or exposures that move from standard classification into NPA status during a reporting period. They measure new stress entering the impaired-loan stock, rather than the total stock of NPAs.

### 9. Why do write-offs matter?

Write-offs remove loans from the reported balance sheet or gross NPA stock, depending on the treatment. They can reduce GNPA without a cash recovery from the borrower. Analysts should review write-offs together with recoveries, upgrades and post-write-off collections.

### 10. How is credit cost calculated?

A common approach is impairment or loan-loss provisions charged during the period divided by average loans or average advances, multiplied by 100. However, companies may use different numerators and denominators. The company's own disclosed definition must be used before peer comparison.

### 11. Why can credit cost rise while GNPA remains low?

Credit cost is a flow measure, while GNPA is a closing stock ratio. A lender may increase provisions for expected losses, macroeconomic uncertainty or portfolio growth even if current reported NPAs remain low.

### 12. What does the FY2026 ICICI snapshot show?

Standalone GNPA declined from 1.73% in FY2025 to 1.44% in FY2026. NNPA declined from 0.42% to 0.35%, while PCR declined slightly from 76.2% to 75.8%.

### 13. What does the FY2026 Bajaj Finance snapshot show?

Standalone AUM grew approximately 20.8% to ₹3,72,986 crore. GNPA increased from 1.18% to 1.27%, while NNPA declined from 0.56% to 0.52%. Stage 3 PCR was 60%.

### 14. Why is Bajaj's consolidated GNPA different from its standalone GNPA?

Standalone financial statements cover Bajaj Finance Limited itself. Consolidated statements include relevant subsidiaries and group entities. The loan portfolios and denominators differ, so the resulting ratios need not be the same.

### 15. What is the difference between GNPA and Gross Stage 3?

GNPA is based on regulatory asset classification. Gross Stage 3 is an Ind AS expected-credit-loss classification for credit-impaired assets. They can differ in recognition, measurement and scope. They should not be treated as interchangeable without a reconciliation.

### 16. How did COVID-19 affect credit-risk measurement?

The moratorium and restructuring framework changed repayment schedules and allowed eligible accounts to retain standard classification under prescribed conditions. This complicates direct comparison of delinquency and NPA ratios around the period. Analysts should examine restructuring, slippages and subsequent recognition.

### 17. Why is IL&FS not purely systematic or idiosyncratic?

The stress originated at a specific institution, making its origin idiosyncratic. Its funding and liquidity effects spread across parts of the NBFC sector, creating sector-wide contagion. It therefore illustrates the transmission from firm-specific stress to broader sector risk.

### 18. How do banks differ from NBFCs?

Banks can accept demand deposits and are subject to banking-specific regulation, while NBFCs generally rely on borrowings, market instruments and other funding sources. Their asset-liability structures, liquidity risks, capital requirements and disclosures differ.

### 19. How can rapid loan growth increase risk?

Rapid growth can stretch underwriting, verification, collections and monitoring capacity. It can also increase exposure to newer borrower segments before sufficient repayment history has accumulated. Growth is not inherently bad, but it must be evaluated against vintage performance, slippages and credit cost.

### 20. Why does correlation not establish causation?

Two variables can move together because of a third factor, common economic conditions or coincidence. Establishing causation requires a defensible identification strategy, appropriate controls and evidence that alternative explanations have been addressed.

### 21. Why is a four-observation regression weak?

Four observations provide very few degrees of freedom and make estimates highly sensitive to any single point. Statistical significance and model reliability cannot be established convincingly from such a small sample.

### 22. Why is share-price performance not the same as credit quality?

Share prices reflect expected earnings, growth, interest rates, liquidity, risk appetite, valuation and many other factors. Credit quality is only one input. A rising share price does not prove improving asset quality, and a falling price does not prove deteriorating credit quality.

### 23. How do mergers affect historical comparisons?

A merger can change scale, portfolio mix, asset base and reporting perimeter. HDFC Ltd merged into HDFC Bank effective 1 July 2023; Shriram Transport Finance and Shriram City Union Finance merged to form Shriram Finance in December 2022. Analysts must identify effective dates, restated comparatives and whether the historical series is pro forma or reported.

### 24. What is the most important research finding?

The most important insight is that credit risk is multidimensional. FY2026 data shows different GNPA, NNPA and coverage movements across the selected institutions. Those ratios must be interpreted alongside growth, provisions, borrower mix, write-offs and funding structure. A sound conclusion requires consistent reporting bases and verified historical data.

### 25. Why can ICICI's FY2026 GNPA ratio differ between the annual statements and Q2 FY2027 performance review?

The FY2026 standalone financial statements report GNPA on a gross-advances basis, while the Q2 FY2027 performance review reports the ratio on a customer-assets basis. The denominator can include different categories such as lease receivables and credit substitutes. The figures should be labelled by basis rather than treated as contradictory.

### 26. How do you reconcile the gross NPA stock?

Opening gross NPAs plus gross additions, less recoveries/upgrades/others, write-offs and sales of NPAs, equals closing gross NPAs. ICICI's FY2025, FY2026 and Q2 FY2027 bridges in the dashboard reconcile exactly. A falling GNPA stock can therefore reflect both lower fresh slippages and higher recoveries/write-offs/sales; it is not by itself proof of better new-loan underwriting.
